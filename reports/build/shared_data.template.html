reimp_shared.hub

{{samples}} samples from {{cases}} patients

Each row of the dataset is one RNA-seq aliquot from one of {{projects}} TCGA projects. The projects differ in size {{project_ratio}}-fold: {{largest_project}} contributes {{largest_samples}} samples, {{smallest_project}} {{smallest_samples}}.

{{primary}} samples are primary tumours and {{normals}} are solid-tissue normals, spread over {{normal_projects}} projects; the rest are metastases, recurrences and blood-derived cancers.

Why every split is drawn by patient

{{multi_cases}} patients contribute more than one aliquot — {{multi_samples}} samples, {{multi_pct}} of the dataset — and {{mixed_cases}} of them contribute more than one kind of sample, typically a tumour and its matched normal. Aliquots of one patient are not independent, so every assignment on this page is a function of the patient alone: all of a patient's samples land on the same side of every split.

reimp_shared.data.load_expression

Six quantifications of every library

GDC's STAR pipeline reports six measures per gene, and each is its own config of the dataset. Three are read counts and three are normalized per library. The loaders pick one with quantification=; the default is raw unstranded counts.

Libraries

A median library holds {{reads_median}} reads, and the 1st to 99th percentiles run from {{reads_q01}} to {{reads_q99}}, a {{depth_ratio}}-fold spread. STAR assigns {{assigned_median}} of a median sample's reads to a gene, and {{protein_share_median}} of those assigned reads fall on protein-coding genes. Depth varies this much from library to library, which is what the normalizing transforms below correct for.

reimp_shared.preprocess.select_genes

{{selected}} protein-coding genes by default

The dataset carries {{genes}} GENCODE v36 genes of {{biotypes}} biotypes. By default the loaders select the protein-coding ones: {{selected_gene_pct}} of the genes, which together carry {{selected_read_pct}} of all assigned reads.

The default selection, step by step:

  1. {{genes}} genes in the dataset.
  2. {{protein_coding}} of biotype protein_coding.
  3. {{selected}} after dropping {{par_y_protein}} _PAR_Y rows — the Y-chromosome copies of pseudoautosomal genes, which GDC quantifies as zero in every sample ({{par_y}} such rows across all biotypes).

How often each gene is seen

Of the {{selected}}, {{always_detected}} have at least one read in every sample and {{never_detected}} in none; a median sample detects {{detected_median}}. The rest spread across the range: genes that are on in some tissues and cancers and off in others.

Other choices go through the same argument: gene_types=None keeps all {{genes}} genes, and gene_ids= takes a fixed list of Ensembl IDs, in its own order, versioned or not. Every choice works with every quantification and transform.

reimp_shared.splits

Five folds, each {{train_pct}} / {{val_pct}} / {{test_pct}}

Every fold divides the patients into train, val and test, and two steps decide which is which. Step 1 maps each patient to one of {{n_buckets}} buckets, in {{n_folds}} blocks of {{fold_width}}: its rank within its project picks the block, so every project fills the blocks evenly, and its hash picks the bucket in the block. Step 2 maps buckets to folds. There is no split file: the same dataset gives the same folds on every machine, whatever quantification, genes or samples are loaded.

From barcode to fold

An aliquot's TCGA barcode names the patient in its first three fields; that case ID is what step 1 hashes.

Step 2 in full

The {{n_buckets}} buckets form {{n_folds}} folds of {{fold_width}}. In fold k, fold k's own buckets are test; of every other fold, the last {{val_buckets}} buckets validate and the rest train. Validation is held out from fitting for choices such as when to stop, and is drawn evenly from every training fold; setting a whole fold aside for it would leave {{whole_fold_train_pct}}% to train on instead of {{train_pct}}%.

Five real patients whose buckets the split tests pin, so that a change to the hash, the ranking or the layout cannot go unnoticed:

Reading a fold

In fold k, train is what a method fits on, val is held out from fitting for the choices made along the way, and test is held out for evaluation. Over the five folds every patient is a test patient exactly once, so pooling the five test sets evaluates the whole cohort; one fold on its own is an ordinary train / val / test split.

reimp_shared.splits.summarize_folds

Every project split evenly

Step 1 ranks each project's patients before dealing them into the blocks, so every project fills every test fold evenly: no project's count in any fold is more than {{max_off}} patients from an even split, even in projects of a few dozen patients. Validation is balanced by project only on average, since the hash alone decides which patients land in each block's last {{val_buckets}} buckets. The band shows how far an unstratified random deal would let a project's share stray; the stratified folds sit on the 20% line.

A χ² test of project against fold gives p = {{chi_projects_p}}. Sample type, which the ranking does not use, splits as a random deal would (p = {{chi_types_p}}).

The price of stratifying: a patient's block depends on how many patients its project has and where it ranks among them. Folds are therefore always computed over the whole dataset, never over a filtered selection, and a dataset release that drops one patient from a project changes the test fold of at most {{stability_max}} others (median {{stability_median}}). Nobody's place within a block moves, since the hash alone sets it.
