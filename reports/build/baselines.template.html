reimp_shared.data.load_expression · reimp_shared.splits

Data and folds

Samples \(i\) are the {{n_samples}} RNA-seq aliquots of {{dataset_short}}; \(p(i)\) is the sample's patient (case_submitter_id) and \(\pi(i)\) its TCGA project. Genes \(g\) range over \(G\), the \(\left|G\right| = {{genes_n}}\) protein-coding genes of GENCODE v36 with the _PAR_Y rows removed, out of {{genes_all}} in the dataset. \(c_{ig}\) is the unstranded STAR read count (config unstranded). Both baselines and the invertibility target start from the log-normalized value

\[ x_{ig} = \ln\left(1 + L\,\frac{c_{ig}}{\sum_{g' \in G} c_{ig'}}\right), \qquad L = 10^{ {{lib_exp}} }, \]

which rescales every library to \(L\) reads over the protein-coding genes before the log (load_expression(transform="lognorm")).

The folds partition patients. Fold \(k \in \{0, \dots, 4\}\) assigns every sample to a training set \(T_k\) ({{train_pct}}% of patients), a validation set \(V_k\) ({{val_pct}}%) or a test set \(E_k\) ({{test_pct}}%), all samples of a patient together, stratified by project; every patient is in exactly one \(E_k\). The assignment is a deterministic function of the salt {{salt}} and is described in the data report. Probes fit on \(F_k = T_k \cup V_k\), the fitting set of fold \(k\).

reimp_shared.eval.baselines

Two baselines, refit in every fold

Both baselines are fit in fold \(k\) on \(T_k\) alone. \(V_k\) is not used: neither baseline has a hyperparameter to select or a point at which to stop. Their training statistics are the per-gene mean and population variance

\[ \mu_g^{(k)} = \frac{1}{\left|T_k\right| }\sum_{i \in T_k} x_{ig}, \qquad \left(\sigma_g^{(k)}\right)^2 = \frac{1}{\left|T_k\right| }\sum_{i \in T_k}\left(x_{ig} - \mu_g^{(k)}\right)^2 . \]
PCA-\(d\) (pca_embeddings)
Let \(V_d^{(k)} \in \mathbb{R}^{\left|G\right| \times d}\) hold the leading \(d\) right singular vectors of the \(\left|T_k\right| \times \left|G\right| \) matrix with entries \(x_{ig} - \mu_g^{(k)}\), \(i \in T_k\). Every sample is embedded as
\[ z_i^{(k)} = V_d^{(k)\top}\left(x_i - \mu^{(k)}\right) \in \mathbb{R}^{d}, \]
without whitening, by sklearn.decomposition.PCA(n_components=d, svd_solver="randomized", random_state=0). It is fit for \(d \in \{ {{dims_set}} \}\); PCA without a subscript means PCA-{{head}}.
HVG-\(n\) (hvg_embeddings)
Let \(H_n^{(k)}\) be the \(n = {{hvg_n}}\) genes with the largest \(\sigma_g^{(k)}\) (ties broken by gene order). Every sample is embedded as the standardized values of those genes, in dataset gene order:
\[ h_{ig}^{(k)} = \frac{x_{ig} - \mu_g^{(k)}}{\sigma_g^{(k)}}, \qquad g \in H_n^{(k)}, \qquad h_i^{(k)} \in \mathbb{R}^{n} . \]
This is the "Identity" representation of Gross et al. (2024), their strongest per-cohort survival representation, deep models included; they ranked genes by the variance of TPM, while here ranking and scaling both use \(x\). Against PCA it measures what projecting onto principal axes gains or loses relative to the most variable genes themselves.

Each fold's baseline embeds all {{n_samples}} samples, and the probes of fold \(k\) read fold \(k\)'s embeddings of \(F_k\) and \(E_k\). The files are out/pca<d>/fold<k>.parquet and out/hvg{{hvg_n}}/fold<k>.parquet, written by reimp-shared baseline-pca and baseline-hvg.

reimp_shared.eval.protocol · reimp_shared.eval.bootstrap

Scoring: out of fold, pooled, patient bootstrap

Out of fold
For each probe and fold \(k\), everything the probe fits — standardization, regression weights, penalty choice, cluster centroids — is fit on \(F_k\) and applied to \(E_k\). Predictions are pooled over the five folds, so each sample is predicted once, by the fold that held out its patient, and each score is one statistic of the pooled predictions, not a mean over folds. The exceptions are marked where they occur: scores of a whole embedding space (effective rank, NMI, ARI) are computed per fold and averaged, and C-index pairs never span two folds.
Standardization
Inside every regression probe, each embedding dimension is standardized over the probe's fitting rows: \(\hat z_{ij} = (z_{ij} - m_j)/s_j\), with \(m_j, s_j\) the mean and standard deviation over those rows.
Intervals (bootstrap_weights, summarize)
Let \(P\) be the scored patients. Replicate \(b = 1, \dots, B\), \(B = {{bootstrap_n}}\), draws \(\left|P\right| \) patients uniformly with replacement (numpy.random.default_rng(0)), and \(w_{ib}\) is the number of times \(p(i)\) was drawn. Every score is a ratio of sums over samples, or over pairs of samples weighted by \(w_{ib} w_{jb}\), so the replicate score \(s_b\) is recomputed from the same predictions without refitting. The interval is the 2.5th to 97.5th percentile of \(s_1, \dots, s_B\). It is conditional on the fitted probes and on the fold assignment; the second is measured under Folds drawn again.
Paired differences (paired_differences)
Two embeddings scored on the same samples receive identical weights \(w_{ib}\). Their difference \(\Delta = s^{A} - s^{B}\) has replicates \(\Delta_b = s^{A}_b - s^{B}_b\), from which the interval is taken, and the share of replicates with \(\Delta_b > 0\) is reported (reimp-shared probe A B --against B).

Scores without an embedding

Each score is drawn beside the value it takes when the prediction ignores the embedding. \(K\) is the number of classes, \(n_c\) the number of samples of class \(c\), and \(n\) the total.

reimp_shared.eval.compare

HVG-{{hvg_n}} minus PCA-{{head}}

For every score with replicates, \(\Delta = s^{\text{HVG}} - s^{\text{PCA}}\) with its paired interval. Of the {{n_paired}} such scores, \(\Delta\) is positive with an interval excluding 0 for {{n_hvg_higher}} ({{hvg_higher_list}}), negative for {{n_hvg_lower}} ({{hvg_lower_list}}), and the interval contains 0 for the other {{n_paired_ties}}. For the four confounder scores a negative \(\Delta\) means HVG carries less of the technical covariate. HVG has {{hvg_ratio}} times PCA's dimensions, which favours it on regression scores; the ridge penalty is chosen per fold for each.

reimp_shared.eval.classification

Cancer type: balanced accuracy {{project_bacc}} over {{project_classes}} projects

Tasks (task_labels)
project_id: \(y_i = \pi(i)\) on tumour samples (every sample type except Solid Tissue Normal), \(K = {{project_classes}}\). tumor_vs_normal: all samples, normal versus tumour. lung, kidney, colorectal, glioma: \(y_i = \pi(i)\) on the tumour samples of LUAD/LUSC, KICH/KIRC/KIRP, COAD/READ and GBM/LGG.
Model (fit_linear_probe)
Multinomial logistic regression on \(\hat z\), fit on the task's samples in \(F_k\):
\[ \min_{W,\,b}\; -\sum_{i \in F_k} \log \frac{\exp(w_{y_i}^\top \hat z_i + b_{y_i})}{\sum_{c} \exp(w_c^\top \hat z_i + b_c)} + \frac{1}{2C}\|W\|_F^2, \qquad C = 1, \]
by L-BFGS with at most 2,000 iterations (sklearn LogisticRegression defaults otherwise); \(\hat y_i\) is the most probable class.
Scores (classification_scores)
Over the pooled test predictions, with \(n_c\) the test samples of class \(c\) and \(\mathrm{TP}_c, \mathrm{FP}_c, \mathrm{FN}_c\) the usual counts:
\[ \mathrm{BA} = \frac{1}{K}\sum_{c} \frac{\mathrm{TP}_c}{n_c}, \qquad F1_c = \frac{2\,\mathrm{TP}_c}{2\,\mathrm{TP}_c + \mathrm{FP}_c + \mathrm{FN}_c}, \]
accuracy \(\frac{1}{n}\sum_c \mathrm{TP}_c\), macro-F1 the mean of \(F1_c\), and weighted-F1 \(\sum_c (n_c/n)\, F1_c\). Balanced accuracy weights every class equally; its chance level is \(1/K\).

Over {{n_tumour}} tumour samples and {{project_classes}} projects, PCA reaches accuracy {{project_acc}}, weighted-F1 {{project_wf1}} and balanced accuracy {{project_bacc}} {{project_bacc_ci}}; PCA-{{min_dim}} reaches {{project_bacc_min}} and HVG {{project_bacc_hvg}}. {{n_early}} of the {{n_tasks}} tasks come within ±{{level}} of their PCA-{{head}} score at \(d = {{early_dim}}\). COAD and READ, which TCGA's colorectal study analysed as one disease, are the hardest pair: PCA reaches {{colorectal_bacc}} {{colorectal_ci}}.

All four measures for PCA-{{head}}:

Beneath each score, its 95% interval. Majority is the accuracy of predicting the largest class, \(\max_c n_c / n\).

reimp_shared.eval.invertibility · reimp_shared.eval.pathways

Expression: \(R^2\) {{inv_r2}} over genes, {{path_r2}} over pathways

Invertibility (invertibility)
The target is \(y_i = x_i \in \mathbb{R}^{\left|G\right| }\), all {{genes}} log-normalized genes, whatever the embedding was computed from. Ridge regression from the standardized embedding,
\[ \hat y_i = W \hat z_i + b, \qquad \min_{W,\,b} \sum_{i \in F_k} \|y_i - W \hat z_i - b\|^2 + \alpha \|W\|_F^2, \]
with one \(\alpha \in \{10^{ {{ridge_lo}} }, \dots, 10^{ {{ridge_hi}} }\}\) (powers of ten) for all genes, chosen by leave-one-out squared error over \(F_k\) (sklearn RidgeCV). Over the pooled test samples \(E\), with \(\bar y_g\) the mean of \(y_{ig}\) over \(E\):
\[ R^2_{\text{pooled}} = 1 - \frac{\sum_{i \in E}\sum_{g}(y_{ig} - \hat y_{ig})^2}{\sum_{i \in E}\sum_{g}(y_{ig} - \bar y_g)^2}, \qquad R^2_{\text{gene}} = \frac{1}{\left|G'\right| }\sum_{g \in G'}\left[1 - \frac{\sum_{i \in E}(y_{ig} - \hat y_{ig})^2}{\sum_{i \in E}(y_{ig} - \bar y_g)^2}\right], \]
\(G'\) being the genes not constant over \(E\). \(R^2_{\text{pooled}}\) weights each gene by its variance; \(R^2_{\text{gene}}\) weights genes equally and has no interval, because a replicate that misses the few samples carrying a gene's variance sends that gene's \(R^2\) far below 0. Per-sample Pearson is the mean over \(i \in E\) of \(\mathrm{corr}_g(y_{ig}, \hat y_{ig})\).
Pathways (pathway_probe)
\(s_{ia}\) is the ssGSEA score of MSigDB 2026.1 Hallmark set \(a\) (\(\left|A\right| = {{n_pathways}}\)) for tumour sample \(i\), computed by tcga2hf from tpm_unstranded with GSVA's ssGSEA and published unnormalized (score_raw). Scores and embeddings are centred on their project's mean over the fitting tumour samples,
\[ \tilde s_{ia} = s_{ia} - \frac{1}{\left|F_k^{\pi(i)}\right| }\sum_{j \in F_k^{\pi(i)}} s_{ja}, \]
the embedding is then standardized, and \(\hat s\) is a ridge regression fit as above. With every sum over tumour samples \(i \in E\):
\[ R^2_{\text{pooled}} = 1 - \frac{\sum_{i}\sum_{a}(\tilde s_{ia} - \hat s_{ia})^2}{\sum_{i}\sum_{a}\tilde s_{ia}^2}, \qquad R^2_{\text{pathway}} = \frac{1}{\left|A\right| }\sum_{a}\left[1 - \frac{\sum_{i}(\tilde s_{ia} - \hat s_{ia})^2}{\sum_{i}\tilde s_{ia}^2}\right]. \]
0 is what the project alone predicts. The scores derive from each sample's own expression, so this probe is invertibility weighted by gene sets, not an independent label.

PCA's \(R^2_{\text{pooled}}\) is {{inv_r2}}, from {{inv_r2_min}} at \(d = {{min_dim}}\); HVG's is {{inv_r2_hvg}}. PCA's \(R^2_{\text{gene}}\) is {{inv_r2_gene}}, and its per-sample Pearson {{inv_pearson}} against {{pearson_base}} for the mean profile. PCA of \(x\) is the rank-\(d\) linear code of \(x\) with least squared reconstruction error, so among \(d\)-dimensional embeddings with a linear readout, PCA-\(d\) is near the ceiling of \(R^2_{\text{pooled}}\). HVG-{{hvg_n}} contains {{hvg_genes}} of the {{genes}} target genes exactly, up to scale; the ridge recovers the rest from them. The Hallmark \(R^2\) is {{path_r2}} pooled and {{path_r2_pathway}} per pathway.

reimp_shared.eval.survival

Survival: C-index {{c_index}} within projects

Samples and endpoint (survival_samples)
One sample per patient: the earliest (lowest sample_index) of type Primary Tumor or Primary Blood Derived Cancer – Peripheral Blood. The endpoint is {{endpoint}} from {{labels_short}}: time \(t_p\) in days from diagnosis and event indicator \(\delta_p\). Patients without a primary tumour sample or without the endpoint are not scored, which leaves {{survival_patients}} patients and {{survival_events}} events.
Model (fit_cox, select_alpha)
A Cox model stratified by project, with its own baseline hazard per project, fit by L-BFGS on the \(n\) fitting patients of \(F_k\):
\[ \ell(\beta) = -\frac{1}{n}\sum_{p:\,\delta_p = 1}\left[\beta^\top \hat z_p - \log \sum_{q \in R(p)} e^{\beta^\top \hat z_q}\right] + \frac{\alpha}{2}\|\beta\|^2, \qquad R(p) = \{q : \pi(q) = \pi(p),\ t_q \ge t_p\}, \]
with Breslow's handling of ties. The penalty \(\alpha \in \{ {{cox_alphas}} \}\) is chosen by {{cox_folds}}-fold cross-validation over the fitting patients (shuffled, seed 0): fits run from the strongest penalty down, each warm-started from the last, scored by the within-project C-index pooled over the held-out folds, and the path stops at the first \(\alpha\) that does not improve it. The risk score is \(r_p = \hat\beta^\top \hat z_p\).
C-index (survival_scores)
Harrell's C over comparable pairs from the same project and the same fold, since each fold's model has its own risk scale:
\[ C = \frac{\sum_{(p,q) \in \mathcal{P}}\left[\mathbf{1}(r_p > r_q) + \frac{1}{2}\mathbf{1}(r_p = r_q)\right]}{\left|\mathcal{P}\right| }, \qquad \mathcal{P} = \{(p, q) : \delta_p = 1,\ t_p < t_q,\ \pi(p) = \pi(q),\ k(p) = k(q)\}. \]
A project's own C restricts \(\mathcal{P}\) to its pairs; the macro C-index is the mean of those over projects with at least {{macro_min_events}} events. Cancer type is constant within every pair, so it contributes nothing, and 0.5 is chance.

PCA's C-index is {{c_index}} {{c_index_ci}}, from {{c_index_min}} at \(d = {{min_dim}}\); HVG's is {{c_index_hvg}}. PCA's macro C-index over the {{survival_projects}} projects with at least {{macro_min_events}} events is {{c_macro}}. The primary-tumour rule, which TCGA-CDR recommends, leaves out the {{metastatic_only}} patients sampled only at metastasis, {{metastatic_only_where}}; SKCM keeps {{skcm_patients}} scored patients and a C-index of {{skcm_c}} {{skcm_ci}}. A metastasis is sampled after diagnosis, so \(t_p\) measured from diagnosis would include time the patient was guaranteed to survive, and for PFI the first progression can precede the sample; shared/EVALS.md quantifies both in SKCM.

Per project, with intervals from the same replicates: for PCA, {{n_proj_above}} of the {{n_proj}} projects with comparable pairs have an interval wholly above 0.5.

reimp_shared.eval.geometry

Geometry: {{prec1_pct}} of nearest neighbours share the cancer type

Embedding and labels
Tumour samples, \(y_i = \pi(i)\). In fold \(k\) the embedding is centred, not scaled: \(u_i = z_i - m_k\), with \(m_k\) the mean over the fitting tumour samples. Similarity is cosine, \(\cos(u_i, u_j) = u_i^\top u_j / (\|u_i\|\,\|u_j\|)\).
Precision@\(k\) (neighbour_precision)
With \(N_k(i)\) the \(k\) fitting tumour samples most similar to test sample \(i\), \(\mathrm{P@}k(i) = \frac{1}{k}\sum_{j \in N_k(i)} \mathbf{1}(y_j = y_i)\), averaged over test samples, for \(k \in \{1, 10\}\).
Clusters (clustering_agreement)
k-means with as many clusters as projects in \(F_k\), on \(u_i / \|u_i\|\) of the fitting samples (sklearn KMeans, n_init="auto", seed 0); test samples take their nearest centroid \(\hat c_i\). \(\mathrm{NMI} = I(y;\hat c) / \left(\tfrac{1}{2}[H(y) + H(\hat c)]\right)\) and the adjusted Rand index over the test samples, per fold, then averaged over folds; neither has an interval, since a replicate's duplicated patients form pairs that agree by construction.
Silhouette and spectrum (spectrum)
The cosine silhouette of the project labels among each fold's test samples, averaged over all test samples. With \(\lambda_1 \ge \lambda_2 \ge \dots\) the eigenvalues of the covariance of fold \(k\)'s test embeddings and \(\rho_j = \lambda_j / \sum_l \lambda_l\), the effective rank is \(\exp\left(-\sum_j \rho_j \ln \rho_j\right)\) and the top share \(\rho_1\), each averaged over folds.

PCA's precision@1 is {{prec1_pct1}}, against \(\sum_c (n_c/n)^2 =\) {{prec_chance_pct}}; NMI {{nmi}}, ARI {{ari}}. PCA-{{head}}'s effective rank is {{eff_rank}} and its first component's share {{top_share}}: principal components are ordered by variance, so a few dominate the spectrum.

reimp_shared.eval.confounders · reimp_shared.labels.technical_covariates

Confounders: technical covariates within projects

Covariates
With \(N_i\) the library's total reads — the unstranded counts over all {{genes_all}} genes plus STAR's unmapped, multimapping, no-feature and ambiguous tallies — and \(c^{(1)}, c^{(2)}\) the first- and second-strand counts:
\[ q^{\text{reads}}_i = \log_{10} N_i, \qquad q^{\text{assigned}}_i = \frac{\sum_{g} c_{ig}}{N_i}, \qquad q^{\text{strand}}_i = \frac{\sum_g c^{(1)}_{ig}}{\sum_g \left(c^{(1)}_{ig} + c^{(2)}_{ig}\right)}, \]
each sum over all genes. The batches are the sequencing plate, the sixth field of the aliquot barcode, and the tissue source site, the second.
Covariate \(R^2\) (within_project_r2)
On tumour samples, covariate and embedding are centred on their project's mean over \(F_k\) as for pathways, the embedding standardized, and a ridge regression fit per covariate. \(R^2 = 1 - \sum_i (\tilde q_i - \hat q_i)^2 / \sum_i \tilde q_i^2\) over the test samples: 0 means the embedding predicts nothing about the covariate beyond the project.
Batch enrichment (batch_enrichment)
With \(R_i\) the fitting tumour samples of \(i\)'s project with a known batch, and \(N_k(i) \subset R_i\) the \(k = {{neighbours_k}}\) most similar by cosine on the project-centred embedding,
\[ e_i = \frac{1}{k}\sum_{j \in N_k(i)} \mathbf{1}(b_j = b_i) - \frac{1}{\left|R_i\right| }\sum_{j \in R_i} \mathbf{1}(b_j = b_i), \]
averaged over test samples: how much more often a sample's neighbours share its batch than its project's samples do.

Lower is more invariant, though not all of it is artefact: sites differ in their patients, and plates in when and from where samples arrived. PCA's \(q^{\text{strand}}\) \(R^2\) is {{strand_min}} at \(d = {{min_dim}}\), {{strand_head}} at \(d = {{head}}\), and {{strand_hvg}} for HVG; its \(q^{\text{reads}}\) \(R^2\) is {{reads_min}} and {{reads_head}}. PCA's plate enrichment is {{plate_pts}} percentage points and its site enrichment {{tss_pts}}.

reimp_shared.eval.protocol

One fold alone: up to {{fold_max_dev}} from the pooled score

A single fold is an ordinary train / validation / test split: its probes fit on \(F_k\) and score only \(E_k\), a fifth of the patients (reimp-shared probe out/pca{{head}}/fold<k>.parquet). The five single-fold scores \(s^{(k)}\) of PCA-{{head}} differ from the pooled score by up to {{fold_max_dev}} ({{fold_max_metric}}, {{fold_max_fold}}), and {{fold_outside}} of {{fold_scored}} fall outside the pooled score's 95% interval. Pooling uses every test patient and every project's full count of events.

reimp_shared.splits · reimp-shared --salt

Folds drawn again

The bootstrap resamples patients under one fold assignment. To measure the variation it leaves out, the assignment is redrawn with the same rule under {{n_redraws}} other salts ({{salts}}), and PCA-{{head}} is refit and rescored under each. For every score with an interval, the ratio of its standard deviation over the {{n_assignments}} assignments to the half-width of its original interval has median {{salt_ratio}}; the largest change is {{salt_max_dev}} ({{salt_max_metric}}), and {{salt_outside}} of {{salt_scored}} redrawn scores fall outside the original interval. Gross et al. (2024) show that a lead on one split often fails to hold on another; a difference between two embeddings no larger than this spread should be read as a tie.

Built by reports/build/baselines.py from {{dataset}} and labels from {{labels}}; split salt {{salt}}, redrawn under {{salts}}; {{bootstrap}} bootstrap replicates; Hallmark pathways.

Rebuild: uv run python reports/build/baselines.py. It fits and scores whatever baseline is missing or stale under out/ first (about 12 minutes from nothing), and --rerun recomputes all of them.
